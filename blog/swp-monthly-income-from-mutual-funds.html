SWP: Create a Monthly 'Pension' from Mutual Funds

By Kartik Ladda · 5 min read · Updated October 2026

Most people know SIP: investing every month. SWP (Systematic Withdrawal Plan) is the reverse. You invest a lump sum and withdraw a fixed amount every month, while the remaining money stays invested and keeps growing.

Who should use SWP?

  • Retirees who want a regular pension-like income
  • People who receive a lump sum from gratuity, a property sale or LIC maturity
  • Parents funding children's monthly college expenses

Why SWP can beat FD interest

  • Tax efficiency: each withdrawal is part capital and part gain, so only the gain portion is taxed. FD interest is fully taxable at your slab rate.
  • Growth potential: the remaining corpus can earn market-linked returns.
  • Flexibility: change the amount or stop it any time.

The golden rule

Keep your annual withdrawal below the fund's expected long-term return, around 5–7% a year for hybrid funds. Then your corpus can last for decades and may even grow.

Example: ₹25 lakh in a balanced advantage fund with ₹15,000 monthly withdrawal (7.2% a year). Check how long your money lasts using our SWP Calculator.

Best fund types for SWP

Balanced advantage funds, conservative hybrid funds and, for shorter needs, debt funds. Avoid small-cap funds for SWP because their volatility can quickly eat into the corpus.
